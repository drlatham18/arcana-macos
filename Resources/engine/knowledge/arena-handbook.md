# MTG Arena orientation

Source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/mtgarena/getting-started
Updated: 2026-08-14

MTG Arena is the digital implementation of Magic: The Gathering. The Home screen shows quests, daily and weekly wins, mastery progress, mail, and current promotions. The Play blade is where you select a queue or event. Decks opens the deck builder. Packs opens owned booster packs. Store handles purchases and daily deals. Profile contains rank, season, and account progress. The gear opens gameplay, graphics, audio, account, and support options.

# New-player path

Start with the tutorial and Color Challenge, then use Starter Deck Duel or bot matches to learn the interface. Jump In is a low-decision way to combine two themed packets into a playable deck. Do not spend wildcards merely because the client offers Craft All: wildcards are scarce and crafting cannot normally be undone. Before entering a paid event, check its format, Best-of-One or Best-of-Three structure, entry currency, win/loss endpoint, and rewards.

# Battlefield controls

The orange Next button passes priority or advances the game. Resolve All yields through all objects already on the stack, but new decisions can still stop the game. Auto Pass asks Arena to pass priority automatically for the rest of the turn, subject to client safety stops. Full Control makes Arena hold priority more often and exposes choices it normally shortcuts; on Windows the default toggle is Ctrl. Holding Shift while passing can pass more aggressively. A blue Done button confirms attackers, blockers, targets, or another multi-selection. Cancel backs out only while the current action remains reversible.

# Stops and priority

Arena normally creates stops when you have a legal action. Click a phase icon near a player's avatar to add or remove a manual stop. A stop on your side applies to your turn; a stop on the opponent's side applies to their turn. Use Full Control or a manual stop before the moment you need to act, because Arena may otherwise shortcut through a point where it believes you have no useful action. Mana abilities normally do not use the stack. Triggered abilities, spells, and most other activated abilities do.

# Casting and targeting

Arena highlights cards and objects it currently accepts. A dim card may be uncastable because of timing, insufficient or wrong-colored mana, an unpaid additional cost, a restriction effect, missing legal targets, or a zone permission. When a card says target, Arena requires a legal highlighted target. Hexproof stops opponents from targeting that permanent; shroud stops all players. Protection prevents targeting, damage, enchanting or equipping, and blocking from the stated quality. Ward allows targeting but creates an additional triggered cost after the target is chosen.

# Combat interface

In declare attackers, select eligible creatures and then confirm. Tapped creatures and creatures with summoning sickness normally cannot attack. Vigilance prevents attacking from tapping the creature. In declare blockers, drag or select blockers and attackers, then confirm. Blocking does not tap a creature. Menace requires two or more blockers. Flying can normally be blocked only by flying or reach. Trample may assign excess damage to the defending player or attacked permanent after lethal damage is assigned to blockers.

# Trigger ordering and replacement choices

When several triggers you control wait to go on the stack together, Arena may order them automatically. Disable automatic trigger ordering in gameplay settings when order matters. Replacement effects such as choosing which event modifies a draw or how a permanent enters can also be auto-ordered; disable automatic replacement-effect ordering when the result matters. The active player's triggers are put on the stack first, then the nonactive player's, so the nonactive player's triggers resolve first.

# Mana and Auto Tap

Auto Tap chooses mana sources for you and previews the selection by highlighting them. Inspect that preview before confirming when colors, utility lands, treasure, snow mana, or leaving mana open matters. You can tap sources manually before casting. Floating mana is shown near the player avatar and usually empties as steps and phases end.

# Zones and card inspection

Your hand is at the bottom, opponent hand at the top, battlefield in the center, graveyards beside libraries, and exile near the same area. Right-click a card to inspect it and related cards, tokens, spellbook, transformed face, or perpetual modifications. A displayed eye icon indicates revealed information. Cards known from an earlier reveal may remain visually identifiable even if their zone is hidden again, but the companion must not infer unknown cards.

# Deck construction and import

Constructed decks normally contain at least 60 cards and no more than four cards with the same English name other than basic lands. Limited decks normally contain at least 40 cards. To import, copy an Arena-format deck list, open Decks, and choose Import. The deck editor marks missing cards and offers Craft All. Sideboard size and access depend on format and match type; Best-of-One may use a reduced functional sideboard for cards that access cards from outside the game.

# Formats on Arena

Source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/mtg-arena/mtg-arena-formats

Standard is the rotating tabletop-parity format. Alchemy is rotating and includes digital-only and rebalanced cards. Historic is a large Arena-only digital format. Timeless allows nearly every Arena card, with a restricted list rather than banning some of its strongest cards. Explorer is the Arena path toward tabletop Pioneer and uses the Pioneer card pool available on Arena. Brawl is a one-on-one singleton commander-style format; Historic Brawl uses the broader Arena card pool. Draft and Sealed are Limited formats with a minimum 40-card deck. Exact legality, bans, event availability, and rotation are time-sensitive and must come from freshly updated official sources or the Arena client.

# Digital-only mechanics

Alchemy and Historic can include digital-only mechanics. Perpetually changes an object's characteristics across zone changes for the rest of the game. Conjure creates a specified card that was not in the starting deck. Seek randomly finds a qualifying card without revealing the other searched cards or changing their order. Spellbooks are predefined lists from which an effect drafts or conjures. Intensity is a persistent numeric value on a card that effects can increase. Rebalanced cards use an Arena-specific version and may differ from their tabletop printing.

# Economy basics

Gold is earned through play and is spent on packs, some events, and cosmetics. Gems can be purchased and can also be earned in some events. Wildcards craft a chosen card of the same rarity. Duplicate protection, Vault progress, golden packs, mastery rewards, store bundles, event prices, and reward structures can change. Treat all exact economy numbers as time-sensitive and verify them in the current client or official Arena pages before spending currency.

# Ranked play

Constructed and Limited have separate ranks. The visible ranks progress through Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond, and Mythic. Rank movement, qualification thresholds, season dates, and rewards can change. The Profile page and current official announcements are the authority for the active season.

# Troubleshooting and logs

If Arena appears stuck, first read the prompt, inspect the stack, and look for a highlighted Done, Submit, Decline, or selection. Escape can close an inspection overlay but should not be assumed to cancel a game action. For client problems, restart Arena, check the official status page, update graphics drivers, and use the repair or reinstall guidance from Wizards support. Detailed Logs can be enabled in Account settings for diagnostics. On Windows, Arena logs are commonly stored below the user profile in AppData\LocalLow\Wizards Of The Coast\MTGA, but the exact layout can change.

# Accuracy boundary

The Arena client decides what actions it accepts. A screenshot cannot establish hidden cards, all continuous effects, every cost choice, or priority history. The companion explains rules and visible interface state; it should say when evidence is insufficient. Current event schedules, bans, card rebalances, prices, rewards, and technical incidents require a fresh knowledge update or direct confirmation in the client.
